# Dispensing mechanism — one rotor per medicine

**Architecture confirmed 2026-08-04: ten rotors, ten OTC medicines, one motor
each. Every pocket in a given rotor holds the same medicine.**

This is the authority for the mechanism. Where `README.md` or
`PRINT-READY/PRINT-THIS.md` disagree, this file wins.

## The mechanism

Two **stations** are fixed in space. The rotor indexes underneath them.

```
                 TUBE (reload)
                      |
                      v
              . . .  [12]  [1]  . . .
                            ^
                            |
                      HOLE (dispense)
                    aperture in the deck

     rotor turns COUNTER-CLOCKWISE, one pocket per index
     a pocket travels:  [2] -> [1] -> [12] -> ...
```

One index step, in order:

1. The loaded pocket arriving at **[1]** sits over the deck aperture. Its
   container **falls through** — the dispense.
2. The pocket that just emptied arrives at **[12]**, under the tube. One
   container **drops in** from the stack — the reload.

Nine pockets, nine doses per revolution. The rotor is full at rest except for
the pocket crossing the hole.

The clock is an analogy only. Nine pockets index at **360 / 9 = 40.000°**, not
the 30° of a clock hour. Never lay anything out from clock positions.

## Why a single stationary tube is the right choice

The abandoned design bolted a magazine above every pocket, turning with the
rotor. It could not meter, and `PRINT-THIS.md` said so:

> the pocket is 16 mm and the container 15 mm, so the tube stacks straight onto
> the pocket and only a 0.8 mm timing window separates one container from the
> whole column

That is unavoidable when the tube rides over its own pocket — nothing ever comes
between the stack and the hole.

**A stationary tube makes the rotor its own shutter.**

| Rotor position | The stack rests on | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pocket aligned under tube | the empty pocket | one container drops in |
| pocket moved away | solid rotor between pockets | column held up |

### The numbers that make it work

| Quantity | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pocket pitch at R57 | 2π(57)/9 = **39.8 mm** | geometry |
| Pocket tangential width | **20.8 mm** | `medicine_rotor_parametric.scad` |
| Land between pockets | 39.8 − 20.8 = **19.0 mm** | |
| Container tangential width | **20.0 mm** | container spec |
| Rotor thickness | **17.5 mm** | vs 15.0 mm container |

A seated container sits entirely below the top face (15.0 in 17.5), so the next
one in the stack has flat material to rest on.

**Known limitation.** The tube bore is 21.2 mm tangential but the land is only
19.0 mm, so the bore is never *fully* covered — about 2.2 mm always overlaps a
pocket. This does not leak, because a 20.0 mm container cannot pass a 2.2 mm
slot. But it does mean the container only begins to enter in the last ~0.8 mm of
alignment. **Dwell at the reload station until the drop completes** — a 15 mm
fall is ~55 ms free, so allow 200–300 ms for friction. `motion_profiles`
already carries `b_dwell_ms` and `return_settle_ms` for exactly this.

## Parts

### The deck plate — was missing, now generated

`medicine_rotor_parametric.scad` cuts the pockets **through**:

```scad
translate([0, 0, -0.5])
linear_extrude(height = rotor_thickness + 1.0)   // through-cut
```

So nothing held a container up. The deck is the floor the loaded rotor slides
on, and it carries the single drop aperture.

`PRINT-READY/MAIN - rotor deck plate.stl`, generated by
`source/deck_plate_generator.py` (edit constants, re-run, reprint):

| Feature | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OD | 180 mm | 4 mm of rim support beyond the 172 mm rotor |
| Thickness | 5 mm | stiffness under nine loaded pockets |
| Centre bore | 12 mm | drive shaft / hub clearance |
| Drop aperture | 39.8 mm radial × 26.0 mm tangential at R57 | pocket is 35.8 × 20.8 — **2 mm and 2.6 mm of clearance per side** |
| Mounting | 4× M4 clearance @ R83 | clear of the pocket circle and the aperture |

The aperture is deliberately the loose feature. The pocket allows ±1.49° of yaw
and a cocked container stands 16.98 mm; the exit must never be what catches it.

Mesh verified watertight: 4580 triangles, **0 non-manifold edges**, outward
normals, Euler −10 (genus 6 = centre bore + aperture + 4 bolts). ~121 cm³,
**~69 g PETG**.

## Indexing — the unsolved part

The rotor must advance 40° per dispense and keep going through 360°. That rules
out a plain hobby servo, which sweeps 180° at most.

`README.md` also records that **84 gear teeth / 9 pockets = 9.333** is not an
integer, so this rotor can never be indexed by counting gear teeth.

**Recommended: a ratchet and pawl driven by the existing servo motion.**

Cut a **9-tooth ratchet ring** — one tooth per pocket, 40° apart. Integer by
construction, which kills the 9.333 problem outright. A servo-driven pawl
advances exactly one tooth per stroke; a second sprung pawl prevents reverse on
the return.

This maps 1:1 onto firmware that already exists. `DISPENSE_ONE` executes
**A → B → A**, and `motion_profiles` stores `position_a_us`, `position_b_us`,
`travel_time_ms`, `b_dwell_ms`, `return_settle_ms`. One sweep = one pocket.

Sizing check:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Index arc at a pawl radius of 60 mm | 40° = 0.698 rad → **41.9 mm** |
| 30 mm servo horn swept 90° | 2 × 30 × sin 45° = **42.4 mm** ✓ |

So a 30 mm horn at 90° drives a pawl at R60 through one 40° index. Use **MG90S
minimum** — `README.md` is right that an SG90's plastic gears strip on the first
jam, and with ten units that is a recurring failure.

**Still to design:** the ratchet ring, the pawl, and the servo bracket. The pawl
geometry depends on how the servo mounts, which is not decided.

## Ten-machine budget

| Part | Each | ×10 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Rotor | ~150 g / 9 h | 1500 g / 90 h |
| Deck plate | ~69 g / ~4 h | 690 g / 40 h |
| Magazine tube (90 mm) | ~34 g / 2.5 h | 340 g / 25 h |
| Containers, 9 per rotor | ~4.5 g each | ~405 g |
| **Total** | | **~2.9 kg, ~155 h** |

Capacity per medicine: **9 in the rotor + 6 in a 90 mm tube = 15 doses**
(21 with a 180 mm tube). Across ten medicines, **150–210 doses** per refill.

## Safety

Use dummy blisters or sealed samples for all mechanism tests. These parts are
not validated pharmaceutical-contact packaging — sealed blisters stay sealed.
Lid retention is friction-plug only with no latch (`README.md` open issue 4);
that needs a snap bead before this is more than a bench demo.
